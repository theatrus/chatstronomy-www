1. Chatstronomy is a telemetry and control integration for telescopes.

Chatstronomy is a chat bot designed for telescope integration. The software monitors a N.I.N.A. imaging session and posts selected status updates, including image captures and autofocus results, to Discord or Matrix. When explicitly approved in N.I.N.A., a local Discord bot can also relay equipment-control slash commands.

You are responsible for installing the software, supplying the Discord bot token or Matrix credentials, and configuring the connection to your equipment. The software runs locally under your control, on your hardware and network. For the self-hosted version covered by these terms, no data is routed through servers operated by us.

The separately managed Chatstronomy Hub coordinates connections across multiple observatories and routes telemetry through a hosted service. Its hosted terms and hosted privacy policy explain the applicable service conditions, data storage, and retention practices.

The source code is published under the Apache License 2.0 at github.com/theatrus/chatstronomy. Those license terms govern the software itself, whereas these terms govern your use of the application.

2. We do not collect or store user data.

The self-hosted application does not communicate with our servers, so we do not collect or store its user information. It does not send developer telemetry, application analytics, or crash reports; require a license check; or create a project-operated account. Observatory updates you choose to share still go directly to your configured Discord or Matrix service.

Local configuration includes N.I.N.A. profile settings, protected credentials in Windows Credential Manager, and a small state file that tracks active chat message identifiers. Removing the application does not necessarily remove its profile, credential-store entries, state, or logs; remove those separately when appropriate. Content already sent to Discord or Matrix is managed by that service. See the self-hosted privacy policy for details.

3. The application transmits telemetry data to third-party chat services.

Ordinary event categories, images, and N.I.N.A. popup notifications start enabled. Review or disable them in N.I.N.A. before starting a local runtime. Disabled event categories never reach the runtime, including previously buffered records; events captured while disabled are not released when a category is re-enabled. No background state-maintenance exception bypasses these settings. Disabling images also prevents image history, thumbnails, previews, and explicit last-image requests. Equipment-status snapshots and other permitted non-image queries can remain available. Raw N.I.N.A. logs are disabled unless you explicitly enable individual log levels.

Permitted updates travel directly to your configured Discord or Matrix service and are governed by that service's terms. We cannot access, retrieve, or delete content posted by a self-hosted runtime. The plugin does not disclose observatory coordinates or location-derived mount data unless location sharing is explicitly enabled in N.I.N.A.; hardware device and driver identifiers remain redacted.

Assume other users may view a shared chat channel or Matrix room, and that its membership can change. Image contents, capture metadata, celestial target coordinates, equipment display names, notifications, and optional logs may still reveal sensitive information. You are responsible for deciding what to send and where to send it.

Your usage of Discord is governed by the Discord Terms of Service, and your usage of Matrix is subject to the terms of your selected homeserver.

4. Telescope control features present security and operational risks.

Hardware control and every individual command are disabled by default. A physical operation requires both the N.I.N.A. hardware-control master switch and that exact command's individual permission. Enabling the master switch alone grants no equipment access.

Monitoring and read-only status commands remain available without hardware permissions. Approved operations can include parking or unparking a mount, changing filters, guiding, camera cooling, autofocus, aborting captures, and starting or stopping imaging sequences. Skipping sequence validation requires its own additional local permission. Disable the master switch to immediately return the N.I.N.A. profile to monitoring-only operation.

Additional safeguards narrow access but cannot override the permissions configured in N.I.N.A.:

  • Local command permissions are mandatory. The master switch and the individual command must both be enabled in the active N.I.N.A. profile. A Discord user, server manager, or role cannot enable these settings remotely.
  • Discord authority is restricted. With an empty user allowlist, only managers of the invoking Discord server can request approved hardware commands. With an explicit allowlist, only those listed users qualify. Requests must come from the telescope's configured channel; direct messages and unrelated servers are rejected.
  • Destructive commands require explicit confirmation. Commands such as parking, aborting a capture, or stopping a sequence require an initial button press confirmation. This mechanism prevents accidental command execution but does not block authorized malicious actions.

These safeguards do not eliminate the fundamental risk: the safety of enabled equipment operations depends on the security of your N.I.N.A. computer, Discord accounts, configured server and channel, and bot credentials. A compromised authorized account can still request commands permitted by both the Discord checks and your local N.I.N.A. settings.

You must evaluate the potential consequences of incorrect command execution on your specific hardware. Unwanted mount movements, such as slewing or unparking, can cause physical collisions, cable wraps, or optical damage. Aborting a capture or stopping a sequence can result in data loss or lost imaging time. The chat interface has no awareness of local physical conditions, such as roof status, daylight, or local personnel.

Enable only the individual operations whose risks you accept. Limit server management access or an explicit allowlist to essential accounts, secure those accounts, and configure independent horizon limits, meridian limits, roof interlocks, and emergency stops. Chat permissions are not a physical safety system.
